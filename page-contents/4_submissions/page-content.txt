  content: '<p>Earthrise Commons will serve as a digital resource companion to our Open Access book, featuring projects that highlight how sustainability education and community engagement are being integrated into courses, programs, and community initiatives worldwide. These contributions will celebrate diverse approaches to sustainability education and offer actionable insights and models that can be adapted by other institutions and communities to enhance learner engagement, equity, and impact.</p><p>Selected contributions are a form of publishing that you can use to build community, advance ideas, and sprinkle some sustainability learning and community engagement into your teaching, no matter your field of study.</p>'

  content: '<h3><strong>By sharing your work, you will:</strong></h3><ul><li><p>Join a local and global community of sustainability educators and practitioners</p></li><li><p>Contribute to open educational resources for sustainability action</p></li><li><p>Connect with peers through our collaborative webinar series</p></li><li><p>Help build a more sustainable and equitable future</p></li></ul><p>We’re transforming a forthcoming anthology, Sustainability Learning for Action &amp; Community Engagement (Palgrave Macmillan SpringerNature), into a living sustainability education platform called the Earthrise Commons. This Open Education Resource will provide educators worldwide (high school through college undergraduate) with free, accessible, adaptable, and shareable content to support sustainability education and action.</p><h3><strong>Our platform includes:</strong></h3><ul><li><p>A Creative Commons License:</p><ul><li><p>CC-BY-NC</p></li><li><p>CC-BY-SA</p></li><li><p>CC-BY</p></li></ul></li><li><p>Original book chapters via Springer Nature Link (with metrics)</p></li><li><p>Lesson plans that educators can modify</p></li><li><p>Interactive resources and videos</p></li><li><p>A toolbox of sustainability competencies and frameworks</p></li><li><p>Student work examples (with permission)</p></li></ul><p>The project uses an open-source infrastructure, managed by a team of curators at SUNY New Paltz, U.S. The design prioritizes search ability, follows UN Global Goals taxonomies, and supports Universal Design for Learning principles. This approach activates sustainability education in a collaborative, interdisciplinary learning space where knowledge can be shared and built upon by the community.</p><h3><strong>Each contribution may also include:</strong></h3><ul><li><p>A personal bio page (optional, this can be as short as two lines)</p></li></ul><p>On your bio page, you may provide:</p><ul><li><p>Links to more resources</p></li><li><p>An indication that you’re open to being contacted about the project</p></li><li><p>An email contact method</p></li></ul>'

    <h2>How to Contribute</h2><h3>Option 1 (Preferred)</h3><p><a href="https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSehPkUSwpni4EtFeF5Bwo34GdIb4vbXefE3X_-ZendN4eXJHQ/viewform" title="https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSehPkUSwpni4EtFeF5Bwo34GdIb4vbXefE3X_-ZendN4eXJHQ/viewform"><strong>Fill out the Google Form</strong></a>.</p><h3>Option 2</h3><p>Use one of the Google Docs below instead.</p><h3>Option 3</h3><p>Already have something made?</p><p>You can simply contribute what you already have:</p><ul><li><p>a lesson plan</p></li><li><p>a workshop idea</p></li><li><p>a classroom activity</p></li><li><p>a resource, such as a video, an essay, an interactive…</p></li></ul><p>Any format is okay.</p><h2>Time this Takes</h2><ul><li><p>Simple/quick options: about&nbsp;<strong>10–15 minutes</strong></p></li><li><p>More detailed formats: take longer</p></li></ul><p>Most of your time will go into creating or sharing a lesson plan. The Earthrise “gardeners” team will help support, organize, and publish your work.</p><h2>Other Things to Do</h2><ol><li><p><strong>Choose a license for your work</strong></p><p>New work uses CC BY (free for anyone to use with credit). Adapted work uses CC BY-NC (free for education, not for commercial use).</p></li><li><p><strong>Introduce yourself (optional)</strong></p><p>You can add a short bio and links to resources, and let people know whether they can reach out to you about your project. They may reach out with questions or suggestions.</p></li></ol><h2>What You Might Include:</h2><h3>Overview</h3><p>What is your initiative? What does it address and why?</p><h3>Case study</h3><p>Walk through a specific example of how it was put into practice and what happened.</p><h3>Equity and Access</h3><p>How does your approach include people with different backgrounds, abilities, or resources?</p><h3>Impacts</h3><p>What outcomes did you see or hope to see?</p><h3>Adaptability</h3><p>How can others tailor this to their own community or classroom?</p><h3>Additional resources</h3><p>List and link to any tools, frameworks, or materials that support your approach.</p><h2>Suggested Topics</h2><p>Contributions can address any sustainability theme.
